FOLIA CODEX — THE READING ROOM
=====================================================
This is a plain text file. Open and edit it with Notepad, TextEdit, or
any text editor — no coding required.

Each block below is one real agriculture/plant-science manuscript, linked
to a Pokémon specimen through your own commentary. This is meant for real
papers you've actually read — nothing here should be a fabricated
citation. Always link to the real DOI, journal page, or a Google Scholar
search for the title so a reader can go verify the source themselves.

OPEN ACCESS ONLY, PLEASE:
The people reading this aren't all scientists — some are just Pokémon
fans who followed a link. A paper locked behind a paywall is a dead end
for them, so only add papers that are actually free to read in full.
Quick ways to check before adding one:
  - Published in a fully open-access journal (Frontiers, MDPI, PLOS,
    Scientific Reports, etc.) — usually free by default.
  - Hosted on PMC (a link containing "ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc" is free).
  - Check unpaywall.org or the "OA" icon on Google Scholar's search
    results before linking a DOI you're not sure about.
If a great paper you want to feature is paywalled, look for an open
preprint version (bioRxiv, SSRN) instead of skipping it entirely — just
note in "connection" that it's a preprint rather than the final version.

HOW TO ADD ONE:
1. Copy the TEMPLATE block below (between the ### TEMPLATE and ### END
   lines) and paste it right above this instructions block ends, anywhere
   in the file.
2. Change "template" on the first line to a short unique id (lowercase,
   no spaces — e.g. "khamare-2022-allelopathy").
3. Fill in each field. Leave "related_specimen" blank if the paper isn't
   tied to one specific Pokémon. Leave "open_access: yes" as-is if the
   paper is free to read in full — only change it to "no" for the rare
   preprint-instead-of-paywalled-final-version case mentioned above.
4. Save the file. Refresh the app in your browser to see it appear.

Do NOT summarize or copy-paste the paper's actual abstract into
"connection" — copyright reasons aside, the "connection" field is meant
to be YOUR idea for how it maps onto the Pokémon world, not a restatement
of the paper.

### cerepaka-schlupp-2023-gynogenesis   [DRAFT — please review]
title: Sperm specificity and potential paternal effects in gynogenesis in the Amazon Molly (Poecilia formosa)
authors: Cerepaka C, Schlupp I
year: 2023
journal: PeerJ, 11:e16118
doi: 10.7717/peerj.16118
open_access: yes
related_specimen:
connection: Not tied to a Grass-type specimen — this one's the real-world grounding for "The Ditto Problem" over on the Grafting Bench page. The Amazon molly needs sperm from a related species to trigger reproduction, but discards that sperm's genes entirely, producing clones of the mother alone. It's the closest real biology gets to how Ditto breeding actually works — and this paper's finding that even that compatibility is limited to certain related genera is exactly what makes Ditto's near-universal compatibility look so extreme by comparison.

### lee-2025-rose-f3-5-h-nhx   [DRAFT — please review]
title: Overexpression of VtF3'5'H and RhNHX Genes Alters Flower Color and Plant Morphology in Transgenic Rose 'Red Farm'
authors: Lee KY, Lee SY, Kim YJ, Choi YJ, Lim SH, Kang YI
year: 2025
journal: Plants, 14(20):3185
doi: 10.3390/plants14203185
open_access: yes
related_specimen: roselia
connection: Real roses genetically lack F3'5'H, the enzyme needed to make delphinidin (the blue-shifting anthocyanin) — this is why no natural rose has ever been blue. This paper is the real grounding for the speculative "blue arm" theory on Roselia's Case File: getting an actual blue shift took inserting a foreign F3'5'H gene AND raising vacuolar pH via NHX genes together, not one change alone. Worth being clear this paper doesn't study Roselia or anything like it — it's cited here only for the real mechanism the theory borrows.
