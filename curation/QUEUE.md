### 1. A sequential MAP kinase cascade regulates mechanical signalling

**Gravitropism & plant sensory biology** · Psychoactive & Sensory-Signaling Flora

Tran HC, Darwish E, Johansson V, Fernandez-Milmanda G, Zhu T, Fonseca de Lima CF, Van De Cotte B, Colcombet J, Vuylsteke M, De Smet I, Goossens A, Van Aken O. — *Nature communications, 17* (2026)

[Read it →](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13324856)

Touch, wind and wounding switch on a MAPKKK3/4/5–MKK4/5–MPK3/6 cascade within sixty seconds, and that single cascade induces around 800 genes — most of the plant's early touch response. Knock out the middle of it and thigmomorphogenesis weakens: the plant stops rebuilding itself in response to being handled.

Fits: the Psychoactive & Sensory-Signaling room, which argues a plant's real senses are stranger than the psychoactive half of its roster. That room currently makes its case on gravitropism and settling starch grains; this is the other sense, with a clock on it.

Weak: Arabidopsis molecular genetics, and the gene names will not survive contact with a placard. What survives is the sixty seconds.

### 2. Woody plant encroachment alters the vegetative and reproductive phenology of a Vereda palm swamp in Cerrado

**Phenology, photoperiod & vernalization** · Generalist Flora

Alves NF, Costa JP, Morellato P, Oliveira PE. — *International journal of biometeorology, 70* (2026)

[Read it →](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13320010)

Drone imagery plus twelve months of ground monitoring in a Brazilian Cerrado palm swamp, measuring how woody encroachment shifts the timing of leafing and flowering in the vegetation it invades.

Fits: the Generalist room, the phenology room, which answers when rather than where.

Weak: this is land-cover change altering a community's calendar, not a plant reading its own. The room is built on degree-days, night length and vernalization — mechanisms inside one organism. A good paper, probably the wrong shelf.

### 3. Onshore Wind Energy Development Causes Localized but Lasting Shifts in Plant Community Composition and Function

**Allelopathy, succession & pioneers** · Pioneer & Fast-Colonizing Flora

Seifert L, Sivertsen K, Rugstad A, Graae BJ, May R, Hagen D. — *Ecology and evolution, 16* (2026)

[Read it →](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13314548)

Vegetation surveyed at increasing distance from access roads at three Norwegian wind plants commissioned 4, 12 and 19 years ago, scored with Grime's CSR strategies. The disturbance signature is local but has not faded after nineteen years.

Fits: the Pioneer & Fast-Colonizing room, which already runs on succession as a sequence of evictions. CSR is the same framework that room uses to take 'pioneer' at its technical value.

Weak: closer to land-use impact assessment than plant strategy. Useful for the nineteen-years figure more than for the mechanism.

### 5. Reintroduction of lignin-modifying enzymes in the brown-rot fungus Gloeophyllum trabeum restores ligninolytic activities but diminishes lignocellulose degradation

**Wood decay, lignin & saprotrophs** · Deadwood & Decomposer-Associated Flora

Ganjoloo N, Li W, Zhang J. — *Fungal biology and biotechnology, 13* (2026)

[Read it →](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13312777)

The strongest of this batch. Brown-rot fungi descend from white-rot ancestors and threw away their lignin-modifying enzymes, relying on a Fenton chemistry instead. The authors put those enzymes back into Gloeophyllum trabeum — ligninolytic activity returns, and the fungus gets measurably *worse* at degrading wood.

Fits: the Deadwood room, directly. That room runs on 'decay has no single clock' and on a living tree walling off rot rather than healing it. This is hard evidence that abandoning the lignin machinery was an optimisation and not a loss — the sort of correction the room's honesty depends on.

Weak: none obvious. It is a molecular microbiology paper, so the placard has to carry the idea rather than the assay.

### 6. Anatomical drivers of organ size and trait coordinated association between pseudobulbs and roots in Dendrobium

**CAM photosynthesis & succulence** · Nocturnal-Function Flora

Zhang FP, Lǚ DQ, Li HR, Zhang SB. — *Botanical studies, 67* (2026)

[Read it →](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13294433)

Seven pseudobulb and thirteen root traits across 37 Dendrobium species, on how epiphytic orchids size their water-storing organs for a canopy where water arrives unpredictably.

Fits: Nocturnal-Function, and it speaks to Cacnea's Water Economy case file — storage capacity and elasticity are exactly the two dials that case is built on, measured here in a completely unrelated lineage.

Weak: an epiphyte's drought is not a desert's. The link to Cacnea is convergence, not shared strategy, and saying so plainly is the honest version.

### 7. Effects of Climate and Water Limitation on Reproductive Traits and Trait Divergence Across Soil Boundaries in a Serpentine-Tolerant Annual Herb

**Metal hyperaccumulation & serpentine soils** · Mineral-Accumulating Flora

Ibañez N, Paul J, Ergastolo A, Suni S. — *Ecology and evolution, 16* (2026)

[Read it →](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13290394)

Greenhouse common garden on Antirrhinum vexillocalyculatum, a serpentine-tolerant annual, testing whether reproductive timing diverges across a soil boundary and whether rainfall sharpens that divergence.

Fits: the Mineral-Accumulating room, which is carried by serpentine ground rather than by its three exhibits.

Weak: tolerance is not accumulation. That room's real subject is metal ending up inside the leaf — the 1,000 µg/g threshold, Pycnandra bleeding nickel latex. This paper is about surviving the soil, not concentrating it, which is a different claim than the room makes.

### 8. Armillaria Species: Biological Complexity, Bioactive Metabolites and Molecular Foundations for Medicinal and Agricultural Applications

**Clonal longevity, grafting & ancient trees** · Ancient & Long-Lived Flora

Duan Y, Huang Z, Yang X, Tian Q, Ye L, Zhang B, Li X. — *Biology, 15* (2026)

[Read it →](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13295948)

A review of Armillaria: the honey fungus that is also the largest and among the oldest clonal organisms known, covering rhizomorph networks, bioluminescence, and its symbioses with medicinal plants.

Fits: two rooms at once. Ancient & Long-Lived is built on two routes to age — lignin and structure, versus keeping a genotype alive clonally — and Armillaria is the clearest living case of the second. The bioluminescence also touches the luminous fungi in Ornamental & Pollinator.

Weak: a review, and pointed at medicinal and agricultural applications, so the longevity material may be thinner than the title suggests. Check before citing it for the age claim specifically.
